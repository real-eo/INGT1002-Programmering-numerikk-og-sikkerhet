### a) 
Given

$$g(x) = \frac{2}{x}$$

We find via algebra that:
$$
\begin{align*}
x &= \frac{2}{x} \\
x^2&=2  \\
x^2-2&= 0 \\
(x+\sqrt{2})(x-\sqrt{2}) &= 0
\end{align*}
$$

when setting $x=g(x)$, which gives us:

`Negative fixed point:` $p_-=-\sqrt{2}$  
`Positive fixed point:` $p_+=\sqrt{2}$

### b)
Given

$$g(x) = x^{129}$$


We can conclude via the multiplicative properties that:
$$p \in \set{{\R}^2 \lor {\R}^3}$$ 

as $0$ and $1$ are the only numbers, which when multiplied, yields themselves. One exception is $-1$, as it yields itself when raised to a even integer power. 

When checking for this, we see that:
$$
\begin{align*}
x &= x^{129}                \\
0 &= \frac{x^{129}}{x}      \\
0 &= x^{128}                \\
\end{align*}
$$

the power is an even number, and therefore can conclude as such:
 
$$p=\set{-1f, 0, 1}$$


### c)
Given

$$g(x) = \frac{8+2x}{2+x^2}$$

We find via algebra that:

$$
\begin{align*}

x &=\frac{8+2x}{2+x^2}          \\
x(2+x^2) &= 8+2x                \\
x^3 + 2x &= 8+2x                \\
x^3 &= 8                        \\

\end{align*}
$$

when setting $x=g(x)$.

Since we're only after the real solutions, we can conclude that:
$$
\begin{align*}
p &= \set{\sqrt[3]{8}} \\
p &= \set{2}
\end{align*}
$$

as the other two solutions are imaginary due to the missing first and second degree terms $cx$ and $bx^2$ along with a negative constant $d$
